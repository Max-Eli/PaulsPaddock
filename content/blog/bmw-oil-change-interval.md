Few maintenance questions cause as much debate among BMW owners as this one. The car's computer says one thing, forums say another, and the stakes are an expensive engine.

## What BMW recommends

Most modern BMWs use Condition Based Service, or CBS. Instead of a fixed mileage, the car estimates when the oil is due based on how it's been driven, and shows that in the instrument cluster or iDrive. In practice the interval often works out to around 10,000 miles or a year, whichever comes first.

That interval assumes the oil BMW specifies and average driving conditions. Think of it as a maximum, not a target.

## Why many owners change it sooner

- **Heat.** Oil breaks down faster at high temperatures. Florida summers, long stretches idling in traffic and constant AC load all add heat.
- **Turbochargers.** Turbos run extremely hot and are lubricated by engine oil. Most current BMWs are turbocharged.
- **Short trips.** Engines that rarely reach full temperature collect moisture and unburned fuel in the oil.
- **Spirited driving and track days.** High revs and heavy loads are hard on oil.
- **Oil consumption.** Some engines use oil between changes, so the level matters as much as the date.

For those reasons, many BMW owners and independent specialists prefer to change oil somewhere around every 5,000 to 7,500 miles. It costs a little more per year, and it's cheap insurance for an engine that's expensive to replace.

## Use the right oil

BMW approves oils against its own Longlife specifications, such as LL-01, LL-04 and LL-17 FE+. Which one your car needs depends on the engine and model year, and it's listed in your owner's manual. The specification matters more than the brand. An oil without the right approval for your engine may not protect it the way it should.

## Don't forget the level

Many BMWs don't have a traditional dipstick. The oil level is checked electronically through the iDrive or the instrument cluster. Check it every few fill-ups and top up with the correct oil if it's low. Running an engine low on oil is far worse than running a little late on a change.

## The bottom line

Use your car's service indicator as the latest you'd ever change the oil, not the plan. For a turbocharged BMW driven through Florida summers, a shorter interval is an easy call.
