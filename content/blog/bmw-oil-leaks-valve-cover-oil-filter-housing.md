Own a BMW long enough and there's a good chance you'll smell burning oil after a drive or spot oil where it shouldn't be. Two leaks account for a large share of it: the valve cover gasket and the oil filter housing gasket.

Neither one means your engine is failing. Both are worth fixing before they cause damage somewhere else.

## Why BMWs leak oil

Many BMW engines from the mid-2000s onward use rubber gaskets and plastic parts that sit right next to a very hot engine. Thousands of heat cycles harden the rubber until it no longer seals, and Florida heat speeds that up. On these engines it's a normal wear item, not a defect in your particular car.

## Leak one: the oil filter housing gasket

The oil filter housing sits on the side of the engine and also routes oil to and from the oil cooler. The gaskets between the housing and the engine block harden and start to seep.

**What you'll notice:**

- Oil on the side of the engine below the filter housing
- Oil on the drive belt and pulleys
- A burning oil smell
- Low oil warnings between services

**Why you shouldn't wait:** this leak sits directly above the accessory drive belt. An oil-soaked belt can swell, slip off its pulleys and shred. On some engines, pieces of a shredded belt can be pulled behind the crankshaft pulley and damage the front main seal, which turns a gasket job into a much bigger repair.

## Leak two: the valve cover gasket

The valve cover sits on top of the engine. On many BMW six-cylinder engines the cover itself is plastic and can crack or warp, and the rubber gasket hardens over time.

**What you'll notice:**

- A burning oil smell, especially after the car has been running a while
- Light smoke from the exhaust side of the engine bay at stops
- Oil around the edges of the valve cover or in the spark plug wells
- Misfires, if oil gets into the plug wells and onto the ignition coils

On some engines the valve cover also houses the crankcase ventilation system. When its internal diaphragm fails, you can get a rough idle, a whistling noise or lean fault codes, and the fix is often a new valve cover.

## What the repair involves

Both jobs mean removing the parts in the way, cleaning the sealing surfaces properly and installing new gaskets, plus a new cover if the old one is cracked. On an oil filter housing job we also look closely at the belt and pulleys, because a belt that has been soaked in oil should be replaced.

## Other leaks worth knowing about

- **Oil pan gasket.** Slow drips from the bottom of the engine. On some models it's a big job because the front subframe has to be lowered to reach it.
- **Coolant leaks** from the expansion tank, hoses or water pump are often mistaken for oil. Coolant has a sweet smell, and BMW coolant is blue.
