Revive. Restore. Reignite. That's how we think about engine work. A major engine problem can feel like the end of the road for a car, but it often isn't. The right answer depends on what failed, the condition of the rest of the car and what it's worth to you.

## Start with a real diagnosis

Before anyone can tell you whether to repair or replace, they need to know what's actually wrong. Engine noises, smoke and warning lights can come from very different causes. A ticking noise might be a lifter, an exhaust leak or a worn timing chain guide. Low power might be a simple boost leak or a damaged turbo.

The tests that answer the question:

- **Compression and leak-down tests** show whether each cylinder is sealing. Low numbers on one cylinder point to a valve or piston ring problem.
- **A borescope inspection** lets you see inside a cylinder without taking the engine apart.
- **Scan data** from the engine computer shows misfires, timing deviations and sensor readings.
- **Oil and coolant checks** can reveal a head gasket leak or unusual wear.

## When repair makes sense

Plenty of serious-sounding problems are fixable without replacing the engine:

- Oil leaks from gaskets and seals
- Cooling system failures caught before overheating did damage
- Ignition and fuel injector problems
- Timing chain and guide replacement
- Turbocharger replacement
- Head gasket repair, when the head isn't warped or cracked

## When replacement makes sense

Some failures make repair uneconomical:

- A spun rod bearing or a seized engine
- A cracked block
- Severe overheating that has warped the head or damaged the block. Most modern engines are aluminum, which is much less forgiving of heat than old iron blocks.
- Major internal damage from a timing failure

In those cases, a good used or remanufactured engine is often cheaper and quicker than a full rebuild.

## Is the car worth it?

Be honest about the rest of the car. If the body, interior, transmission and suspension are in good shape, a replacement engine is a big repair but can be good value compared with buying another car and inheriting its unknown problems. If the car needs several other major repairs too, it may be time to move on.

## Prevention is much cheaper

Most catastrophic engine failures come with warnings: an overheat that was driven through, an oil level that was allowed to drop, a leak that was ignored for a year. Regular [oil service](/services/oil-change-maintenance), cooling system maintenance and fixing leaks early prevent most of them.
