Wheel alignment is easy to put off because the car still drives. But a car that's out of alignment wears through tires faster, wastes fuel and doesn't handle the way it should.

## What an alignment actually adjusts

Alignment is about the angles of the wheels relative to each other and to the car. Three angles matter most:

- **Toe** is whether the fronts of the tires point slightly in or out, seen from above. Too much toe in either direction scrubs the tires as they roll, and it's the most common cause of alignment-related wear.
- **Camber** is the tilt of the tire in or out at the top, seen from the front. A little negative camber helps grip in corners. Too much wears the inside edge.
- **Caster** is the angle of the steering axis, seen from the side. It affects steering weight and how well the wheel returns to center.

A four-wheel alignment measures and adjusts all four wheels, which matters on cars with independent rear suspension, including nearly every BMW.

## Signs you need one

- The steering wheel isn't centered when you're driving straight.
- The car drifts or pulls to one side on a flat road.
- The tires are wearing unevenly, especially on the inside or outside edge.
- The tires squeal in gentle turns.
- The steering feels loose or the car wanders.

A vibration at highway speed is usually a wheel balance problem rather than alignment. [Balancing](/services/tires-alignment) fixes that.

## When to get one, even without symptoms

- When you fit new tires
- After any suspension or steering work
- After hitting a big pothole or a curb
- After lowering the car or installing coilovers

## Why it matters more on performance cars

Many performance cars, including a lot of BMWs, run wide tires with more aggressive factory camber settings. Small alignment errors show up quickly as uneven wear on expensive tires, and lowering the car makes it worse.

## Alignment needs healthy suspension

An alignment only holds if the parts holding the wheels are in good shape. Worn control arm bushings or tie rods let the wheels move around, and the settings won't stay in spec for long. A proper alignment starts with checking for worn parts.
